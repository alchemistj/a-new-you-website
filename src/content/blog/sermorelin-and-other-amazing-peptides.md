Peptides are a class of chemically active substances linked together by amino acids using peptide bonds.[http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) They have specific physiological duties, including turning white fat into brown fat. Peptides work as signal molecules regulating the Neuroendocrine and metabolic system. They have been widely used in the treatment and prevention of metabolic diseases with low toxicity and low side effects.

Several peptides have become the focus of research for the potential treatment of metabolic diseases, such as diabetes and hyperlipidemia. Neuropeptide Y, adrenomedullin 2, atrial natriuretic peptide, and brain natriuretic peptide to name a few.

Peptides can further be divided into endogenous or exogenous with endogenous, being important regulators of biological processes, such as promoting energy, metabolism, and inhibiting insulin resistance.

Peptides serve an important role in inflammation, tumor development, metabolic diseases, nervous system diseases and circulatory system diseases. They function primarily through receptor binding proteins interaction and hormone activation.

Recently, several peptides have come in to consideration for the long-term treatment of obesity. [https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7604735/table/tII-etm-0-0-09364/](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7604735/table/tII-etm-0-0-09364/)
